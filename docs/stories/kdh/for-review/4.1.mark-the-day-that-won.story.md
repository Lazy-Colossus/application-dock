# Story 4.1: Mark the day that won

## Status

Ready for Review

## Story

**As** an admin,
**I want** to mark a day as the one we are doing, and unmark it if plans change,
**so that** everyone can see the decision on the calendar instead of in a message thread.

## Acceptance Criteria

1. **`PUT /api/kdh/calendars/{id}/chosen`** with `{ date, chosen }` adds or removes the date, keeping `chosen_dates` sorted and deduped, refreshing `updated_at`, and leaving votes untouched. (FR-17)
2. **Any number of days** coexist — a long-running calendar accumulates them. (FR-17)
3. **In the day's own context.** The control lives in the day sheet, not a separate mode, and the cell updates immediately. (FR-17)
4. **A shape, not a colour** — a diamond that survives every wash step and past-day dimming. (FR-17, NFR-6)
5. **A past day may be marked** — unlike a vote, this is a record and may be corrected. (FR-16, FR-17)
6. **Guests see chosen days but cannot change them**; the control is absent, and the API returns `403`. (FR-3)
7. **A malformed date** is `422`; an unknown calendar `404`. Unmarking a day never chosen is harmless.
8. **Serialized** inside `repo.calendar_transaction`. (NFR-1)

### Completion Notes

- **This story broke two rules Epic 3 had just established, and both were wrong.**
  See the decision log, entry 16.
  1. Story 3.5 made a past cell `disabled`, so AC 5 here was unreachable. A past
     day now **opens** but cannot be **answered** — and it turns out showing who
     came to a past session is the point of keeping past votes at all.
  2. Story 3.2 had an unclaimed tap open the name dropdown instead of the sheet,
     which made the sheet unreachable for an admin who has not claimed a name —
     likely, since an admin need not be an invitee. The sheet now always opens and
     the claim prompt sits inside it, in the context of the day just tapped.
- Three Epic 3 tests correctly failed on those changes and were rewritten to the
  corrected rules rather than loosened. A fourth had become vacuous — it still
  passed, for the wrong reason — and was rewritten too.
- `set_chosen` takes a set, mutates it, and writes back sorted: dedup and ordering
  fall out rather than needing to be maintained.
