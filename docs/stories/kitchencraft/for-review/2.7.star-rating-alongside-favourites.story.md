# Story 2.7: A star rating alongside favourites

## Status

Ready for Review

## Story

As a cook,
I want to rate a recipe out of five as well as flag it a favourite,
so that I can tell "this one works" apart from "cook this again soon".

## Context

Story 2.6 shipped favourites carried on a **star** glyph. This story introduces a
separate 1–5 **rating**, which conventionally owns the star. The two cannot share
one mark, so the glyphs swap:

- **Heart** — the favourite toggle. Same behaviour as the star had in 2.6: one
  tap, optimistic, favourites-first ordering, favourites-only view.
- **Stars** — the new rating. Five of them, 1–5, independently settable and
  clearable.

The favourite is *not* derived from the rating and the rating is *not* derived
from the favourite. A 5-star recipe need not be a favourite; a favourite need not
be rated at all. They answer different questions and both are optional.

## Acceptance Criteria

1. **Rating is optional and clearable.** Given any recipe, when it is rated, then the value persists as an integer 1–5; an unrated recipe carries `null`, and re-tapping the current rating clears it back to `null`. No recipe is ever required to carry one.

2. **Favourite moves to a heart, behaviour unchanged.** Given a recipe row, when the heart is tapped, then the favourite flag toggles exactly as Story 2.6 specified — optimistic, no navigation, no scroll movement, reverting on a failed write — and announces as *"Favourite, on"* / *"Favourite, off"*. Every AC of Story 2.6 continues to hold with the new glyph.

3. **The two are independent.** Given a recipe, when it is favourited, then its rating is untouched; when it is rated, then its favourite flag is untouched. Neither is computed from the other, and clearing one leaves the other intact.

4. **Ordering is unchanged.** Given a collection, when it is listed, then the order remains favourites-first with creation-date descending inside each group (FR-10, FR-13). Rating does not enter the sort — a rating-based sort is out of scope here and belongs with the sorting story that does not yet exist.

5. **Both controls fit a phone.** Given a row at 375px, when it renders with a name, a meta line, a heart and five stars, then no control falls below the touch target the rest of the app uses, nothing overflows horizontally, and the recipe name stays readable. The heart takes the trailing edge the star held; the rating sits on the meta line, where there is horizontal room.

6. **Carried in ink, by shape.** Given either mark, when it renders, then it is ink with a shape change — filled versus outlined — never moss and never a colour change alone (UX-DR17). A rating announces as *"Rated 3 of 5"*, and *"Not rated"* when absent.

7. **Absence renders as absence.** Given an unrated recipe, when it appears in the list, then the rating control shows five outlined stars and no count, placeholder, or wording implying the recipe is unfinished (NFR-8).

8. **Rejected at the field and at the API.** Given a rating outside 1–5 or a non-integer, when it arrives at the API, then it is rejected with `{ detail }`, translated from the service's `ValueError` — the same path `total_time_minutes` takes (Story 2.1, AC 2).

### Completion Notes

- **The glyph swap was the whole risk, and it landed as predicted.** Seven existing specs failed on the rename; every one was *retargeted* to the heart rather than deleted, so the behaviour Story 2.6 pinned down is still asserted. `RecipeRow.spec.ts`'s shape test needed `find(".kc-heart svg")` rather than `find("svg")` — the row now renders five rating svgs before the heart, so the unscoped query was silently taking a rating star.
- **`setRating` deliberately does not re-sort**, which is the one way it differs from `toggleFavourite`. A spec asserts the collection order is byte-identical across a rating write, and a backend test asserts the same server-side.
- **It is also outside `loading`**, for the same reason 2.6 documented: the user may be mid-scroll and a spinner has no business reaching them for a one-tap mark.
- **Pydantic coerces before the service sees the value.** `"3"` arrives as `3` and `True` as `1`, so `_rating`'s type guards are unreachable from the API — they are live only for direct service calls, which is how Epic 4's enrichment would arrive. This is pre-existing app-wide behaviour that `total_time_minutes` already has; the tests follow the convention Story 2.1 set, asserting the `{ detail }` string only for values pydantic cannot coerce away, plus a separate test that a fractional rating is rejected by the schema.
- **The star target is 36px wide, not the 44px touch token.** Five full-width targets plus the heart do not fit beside a name at 375px. The stars are contiguous — no dead space between them — so a miss lands on a neighbouring star rather than on nothing, and 36px is well clear of the 24px floor. The height stays at the full token. This is the deliberate deviation most likely to be questioned in review.
- **The row's structure changed**: `.kc-row__main` was the button and is now a wrapper; `.kc-row__open` is the button carrying the name. The rating had to sit outside the element that navigates.
- A `RecipePage` spec that enumerated every button's text now filters empty strings, because the rating stars are icon-only buttons and carry none.

### Amendment (post-implementation)

- **AC 5's placement was overridden by request.** The rating was specced onto the meta line because five touch-sized targets plus the heart do not fit beside a name at 375px. It has since been moved up to share the first line with the recipe name, leaving meal type and time alone on the second. The constraint that drove AC 5 is unchanged, so the line **wraps** rather than truncating: on a narrow phone with a long name the stars drop to their own line, and a clipped recipe name never happens. The rest of AC 5 — no control below the touch minimum, no horizontal overflow — still holds.

## Dev Notes

**The glyph swap is the risky part of this story, not the rating.** Story 2.6's
specs assert on the star; they must be updated to the heart while continuing to
assert the *same behaviour*. A spec that is deleted rather than retargeted is a
regression in disguise.

**Two interactive controls on one row is the design tension.** The row already
carries a name, an optional meta line and one target. Five more targets is the
reason AC 5 exists and the reason the rating goes on the meta line rather than
the trailing edge — a five-star control at full touch size plus a heart does not
fit beside a name at 375px.

**Rating is deliberately outside the sort.** Adding it to the ordering would make
three competing sort keys with no user control over any of them. The sorting
story does not exist yet; when it does, rating is a candidate key there.
