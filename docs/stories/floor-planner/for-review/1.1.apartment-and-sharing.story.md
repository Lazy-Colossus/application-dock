# Story 1.1: The Floor Planner app, one shared apartment

## Status
Ready for Review

## Story
**As** Jake or Dani,
**I want** Floor Planner in the dock, opening on an apartment we both see,
**so that** whatever one of us draws or arranges, the other finds it there too.

## Acceptance Criteria
1. A "Floor Planner" card appears on the landing page, and `GET /api/apps` lists it. Its id is
   `floor-planner`, its icon is `square_foot` (the same icon in both places), and it opens
   `/floor-planner`. Every route requires a signed-in user.
2. A user with no apartment gets an empty one that they own. Its plan is 10 × 8 m (50 × 40
   squares), unlocked, with no furniture and a single "Layout A". It is only saved to disk on the
   first write (FP-1).
3. The owner can add a dock user as a member, any member can leave, and the owner can remove
   members. The refusals are those in SH-1: an unknown user, a user who is already a member, a
   user on another apartment that has other members, a user who owns a non-empty apartment, and
   the owner leaving while others remain. Someone who leaves starts again with a fresh empty
   apartment. Membership is managed from a dialog opened from the avatars in the top bar.
4. The top bar's **Lock plan / Unlock** button works, and the lock is shared by both users
   (FP-8; refusing to paint on a locked plan comes in 1.2). Lock and unlock are the first writes
   to the apartment. Every such write sends `base_rev`. If the stored `rev` has moved on, the
   write is refused with 409. The client then reloads the apartment and says "<name> changed
   this, reloaded" (NFR-a).
5. The page shows design B's frame: the top bar with the Draw plan · Furniture · Arrange switch,
   the lock chip and the avatars, the three columns, and the bottom status bar. The panels can be
   empty for now. A light `app-bar--floor-planner` header variant keeps the dark shell bar off the
   light page.
6. `pytest`, `npm test`, `black`, `ruff` and `eslint` all pass.
