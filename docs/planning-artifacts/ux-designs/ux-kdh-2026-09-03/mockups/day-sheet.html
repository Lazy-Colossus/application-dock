Three day sheets, and the question the current one cannot answer

Monday 14 September on Ta'ilar Ana: Jakub, Lenka and Ada are free, Erika only if needed, Dusan is free, and Dani has not answered at all. Every version below shows that last fact. The one in the app today does not.

What is actually wrong with it

The sheet lists only the people who answered. Open the 14th today and you see five names — and you cannot tell whether the sixth said no or simply never looked. "Who still hasn't voted?" is the question a group asks constantly while trying to settle a date, and this is the surface that should answer it.

Free and if-needed differ only in italics and a trailing caveat. That was carrying weight when each person also had a colour; with the colours gone it is the only distinction left, and it is the faintest one available. It should differ in kind — a shape, a group, a position — not in slant.

Sections
Three headed groups with counts. The distinction is structural, not typographic — and the people who never answered finally have somewhere to appear.

My recommendation

Ledger (B), with the summary line kept. Reasons, in order of how much they matter:

  • Everyone is listed, always. That is the actual fix. Silence becomes visible, and "who do I still need to chase" is answerable without leaving the sheet.
  • Roster order never changes. You open a day looking for one person as often as you open it to read the whole roster, and grouping makes that a search rather than a glance. On a phone especially, stable position beats sorted position.
  • The glyph is a shape, so free / if-needed / silent differ in kind — the same principle that fixed the chosen day. Filled, half-filled, hollow. Italics can stay as reinforcement, but they stop being the only signal.
  • Your answer buttons carry the same glyphs as the rows, so the control and the list speak one language instead of two.

What I would take from the others: the counts from A, as the one-line summary already in B; and nothing from C — the bar is genuinely handsome, but the cell you tapped to get here already told you how good the day is, and repeating it is decoration.

One thing to decide, because it changes the backend: "no answer" and "can't" are the same thing in the data — absence. If you want the sheet to distinguish declined from never looked, that needs a third stored status, which means a schema change and a migration. My advice is not to: for six friends, silence and no are close enough, and the row can simply read "no answer".
