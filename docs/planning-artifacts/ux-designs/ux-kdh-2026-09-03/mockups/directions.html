The ramp

Seven steps for a six-person calendar. The scale is relative to the number of active invitees, so it re-computes when someone is added or removed.

The date and the count stay in every cell. Four and five are close by wash alone — that's the accepted cost of this encoding, and the number is what settles it. The 12th and 15th carry a lilac hairline: full coverage that leans on an if needed, so an admin can see what they're about to ask of people. The 14th is chosen — a small diamond, a shape rather than a colour, so it reads at any point on the ramp and through the past dimming.

Invitee colours leave the purple family

Two different things are being said in colour, and they must not be confused. The ramp answers how many can come — purple, ordered, low to high. An invitee colour answers which person — categorical, no order at all. If people were purple too, someone's colour would read as a coverage level. So the roster runs on varied pastels, desaturated enough to sit on the violet ground without competing with the ramp.

Claiming a name

Nobody has an account. You arrive at a shared link and say which of these people you are, and the app remembers it on that phone. Until you do, the month is fully readable but nothing is tappable — so this control is the first thing a newcomer meets, and the thing a returning voter should barely notice.

The three-state control is explicit here rather than a cycling tap, because this sheet is where a person deliberately answers. On the grid itself a tap still cycles none → free → if needed → none for quick passes down a month. Kit's row shows the if needed treatment: italic and slightly recessed, distinguished by weight and style rather than by tinting her colour — her colour has to keep meaning "Kit".
