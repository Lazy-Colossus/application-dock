Three ways to mark a day without touching the wash

Same September, same votes: the 14th is chosen, today is the 3rd, and the 1st and 2nd are past. What differs is where each mark lives — and none of them paints on the cell's background, because that background is already saying something.

Why this has been hard

The cell interior has two jobs already: its background is the ordered wash (how many can come) and its text is categorical (who). Gold numbers, glows and rings have all been competing for a surface with no room left — which is exactly why they read as loud rather than considered.

Chosen, today and weekend are not facts about coverage. They are facts about the day. So they belong in the channels the wash does not use: the gutter between cells, the cell's frame, and elevation. That reframing also answers the weekend question for free — a signal drawn in the gutter cannot disturb vote information, because it never touches a cell.

Marks live in the mount, never on the picture. A gold frame around the chosen day, a bar under today's number, and a tinted band behind the weekend columns.

My recommendation

Take the chosen-day mark from A, today from B, and the weekend from C. They are independent channels, so they mix — and each is the strongest answer to its own question:

  • Chosen — A's frame. A ring in the gutter is the only mark that looks identical at wash-0 and wash-6. Keep the crown, drop the gold number and the glow entirely: with a gold frame around the cell, the number no longer has to carry the message, so it can go back to normal ink and be readable again.
  • Today — B's inverted disc. Filling the number's own shape is louder than any outline, costs no colour, and cannot be confused with the frame that now means "chosen".
  • Weekend — C's gutter underline. It never touches a cell, so the wash is untouched by construction; and because it is a line rather than a fill, it cannot be mistaken for a coverage step.

That gives three marks that are different in kind — a frame, a filled shape, a line — rather than three variations on colour. Which is the real fix: they stop competing because they are no longer speaking the same language.
