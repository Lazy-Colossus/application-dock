# KDH UX — Decision Log

Canonical record of what was decided and why. `DESIGN.md` and `EXPERIENCE.md`
are distilled from this at Finalize; both win over any mock on conflict.

**Stakes:** hobby — six friends, self-hosted, no compliance surface.
**Mode:** Create. Workspace bound 2026-09-03.

---

## Entry 1 — Workspace naming deviates from the skill pattern

The configured `run_folder_pattern` is `ux-{project_name}-{date}`, which would
give `ux-application-dock-2026-09-03` and read as a second platform-wide run.
This session is app-scoped, so the folder is `ux-kdh-2026-09-03`, matching how
`epics-kdh.md` is named. Pattern deviation is deliberate.

## Entry 3 — FINDING: the "platform spine" is not a platform spine

`ux-designs/ux-application-dock-2026-06-10/` is named for the platform but both
its files are titled **Hotaru** and describe **Neon Yūgure** — a deep-indigo
dusk field with cyan/magenta/violet neon, violet glass panels, drifting
fireflies, **phone-viewport only, full-bleed**. That is one app's identity, not
the dock's.

**KDH therefore inherits nothing from it.** What KDH actually inherits is the
shell's own theme, which lives in code rather than in any UX document:

`frontend/src/css/quasar.variables.sass` — **"Carbon"**
| Token | Value | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `surface-base` | `#141414` | near-black page |
| `surface-raised` | `#1E1E1E` | raised surface |
| `surface-card` | `#242424` | card fill |
| `ink-primary` | `#F0F0F0` | body text |
| `ink-secondary` | `#8A8A8A` | secondary text |
| `ink-disabled` | `#4A4A4A` | disabled |
| `accent` (= `primary`) | `#C8960A` | muted gold — the single accent |
| `border-color` | `#2A2A2A` | hairline borders |
| `confirmed` / `positive` | `#2E7D32` | success |
| `error` / `negative` | `#CF6679` | error |

Consequence for this session: the coverage heat ramp has to live inside a
**near-black, single-gold-accent** world, not a light one. Any palette decision
is made against `#141414`, and gold is already spoken for as "the interactive
accent" across the dock.

## Entry 4 — GAP: form factor was never specified

The KDH design spec states no form factor. The two readings in it pull in
opposite directions: "unmistakable across a room" implies a wide screen, while
six people voting from a shared link will realistically do it on phones. Every
other decision in Epic 3 — how many names fit in a cell, whether `+N` overflow
is the common case or the exception, whether the heat ramp or the count is the
primary signal — depends on this. Must resolve before IA closes.

Note: Listies pinned this explicitly (NFR-1, desktop/wide-first). KDH did not.

## Entry 5 — DECIDED: phone-first, one layout

Everyone does everything on a phone, admins included. One layout, no desktop
variant. Matches Hotaru's form factor and the reality of six people opening a
link from a group chat.

## Entry 6 — CONSEQUENCE: FR-13 as written cannot hold

FR-13 says *"Each day cell lists, in small text, the names of the invitees on
it"*, with a `+N` remainder above an overflow threshold. That was written
assuming a wide cell. A phone month is seven columns across ~360px — roughly
**48px per cell**. Names do not fit; even one 4-letter name at a legible size
is marginal, and six is impossible.

So on a phone the `+N` overflow is not the exception, it is **always** the case,
which makes the whole names-in-cell mechanic dead weight.

**Resolution to confirm with the user:** the cell carries the signals that
survive at 48px — the date, the coverage **count**, and the **heat** — and the
names move to a **day detail** surface opened by tapping the cell, which is
also where a claimed person sets their own availability. This turns FR-13 from
"names in the cell" into "names one tap away", and adds a surface the IA did
not previously have.

Knock-on: NFR-6's *"unmistakable across a room"* is a desktop idea. On a phone
the bar is *readable at arm's length*, one-handed, outdoors.

## Entry 7 — DECIDED: its own identity, but very minimal

Not the dock's Carbon theme, and not a Hotaru-scale expressive world. KDH gets
its own visual language whose defining quality is **restraint** — few elements,
generous space, and colour used sparingly enough that the heat ramp is the
loudest thing on the screen.

Open: field colour (light or dark), and how coverage is expressed. Being
elicited via rendered directions rather than described in prose.

## Entry 8 — DECIDED: the "Wash" encoding

Coverage is the cell's background; the month reads as a field where the good
days are brighter. Chosen over "Fill" (a vessel filling from the bottom) and
"Ring" (a dial per day).

Accepted trade-offs, recorded so they are not rediscovered as bugs:
- Four-of-six and five-of-six are hard to separate by wash alone. **The count
  stays in the cell** to settle it — it is not decoration.
- Wash leans hardest of the three on colour, so the count and the chosen-day
  shape are what carry the meaning for a colour-blind reader.

## Entry 9 — DECIDED: purple world — dark ground, eggplant → pastel ramp

Wash re-skinned at the user's direction: violet-black field, the ramp running
from deep eggplant at low coverage to pastel lilac at full.

## Entry 10 — CONSEQUENCE: the invitee palette must leave the purple family

Two different things are encoded by colour and they must not be confused:
- **the ramp** answers *how many can come* — purple, ordered, low to high;
- **an invitee colour** answers *which person* — categorical, unordered.

If invitees were also purple, a person's colour would read as a coverage level.
So invitee colours become **varied pastels** — rose, sky, mint, sand, lilac,
aqua — desaturated enough to sit on the violet ground without competing with
the ramp for attention.

This obsoletes the 12-hue palette currently shipped in
`kdh_service.PALETTE` (ember, cobalt, violet, jade, magenta, amber…), which was
picked before any visual direction existed and is too saturated for this world.
Replacing it is a small change to one tuple, but it is a change to code already
merged in Story 1.3 — noted for Finalize.

## Entry 11 — DECIDED: header dropdown for claiming a name

Chosen over the bottom sheet (biggest targets, but covers the month) and the
chip rail (never hides the roster, but eats permanent vertical space and wraps
badly past ~8 people).

Consequences:
- The month stays visible while picking — the reason it won.
- It is the natural home for the colour swatches, so **FR-9 recolouring lives
  inside the same control** rather than needing a surface of its own.
- Targets are the smallest of the three, so the rows must be at least 44px and
  the control must not sit flush against the top edge.

## Entry 12 — ASSUMPTION: low end of the ramp needs more separation

Not confirmed by the user. Steps 0 (`#221B2E`) and 1 (`#2B2040`) are close
enough that "one person is free" may read as "nobody". Step 1 is lightened to
`#31234A` in the spine so the first vote is visibly a change; the count is the
backstop either way. Overturnable.

## Entry 13 — Upstream reconciled to the spines

The user ruled that this session's decisions take precedence over the documents
written before it. Changes made:

**`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-02-kdh-design.md`**
- **NFR-0 added** — phone-only, one layout. The spec previously stated no form
  factor at all; this is the constraint the rest follows from.
- **FR-13 rewritten** — names moved from the cell to the day sheet.
- **NFR-6 rewritten** — "unmistakable across a room" became "at arm's length,
  one-handed, on a phone", plus the explicit 4.5:1 contrast floor.
- *Interaction design* and *Visual direction* rewritten to match, both now
  deferring to the spines as the winning contract.
- Frontend structure: `InviteePanel` → `NameDropdown`, `AdminBar` → `AdminMenu`,
  and `DaySheet` added.

**`docs/planning-artifacts/epics-kdh.md`**
- Same FR-13 / NFR-6 / NFR-0 changes; UX spines added to `inputDocuments`.
- **Story 3.3 rewritten** from "See who is on each day" (names in the cell) to
  "The day sheet — who is on this date".
- Stories 2.3 and 2.4 now specify the header dropdown and put the colour
  swatches inside it; 3.1 records arrows-only; 3.2 says the cell carries date and
  count only, and that an unclaimed tap opens the dropdown; 3.4 and 4.1 name the
  actual tokens and marks.
- `useHeatScale` → `useWashScale` throughout, matching the identity.

**`backend/app/services/kdh_service.py`**
- `PALETTE` replaced: twelve saturated hues → the eight pastels, with a comment
  recording that invitee colours are categorical and must stay outside the wash's
  purple family. Backend 863 and frontend 1131 stay green — the palette's tests
  assert uniqueness, ordering and exhaustion rather than specific values, so they
  covered the swap without modification.


## Entry 14 — Roster visibility raised, at the user's direction (2026-09-03)

Change request after seeing the app running:

1. **The name control is far more prominent, and reads as unanswered.** Unclaimed
   it is a negative-bordered pill with a warning glyph — a required field left
   blank — because the calendar is read-only until it is answered, and the previous
   quiet text link did not say so.
2. **The calendar page shows a headcount** beside the calendar's name.
3. **The calendar list row shows the headcount beside the name** (person icon +
   number) and moves **the invitees' names** into the subtext where the count used
   to be, trailing off past the sixth.

Consequence: `CalendarSummary` gains `invitee_names`, so the list can show who is
invited without a request per row. This supersedes part of Story 1.3's AC 7, which
specified the summary as id / name / invitee count only.

Note: the negative colour used for the unanswered state is the dock's Carbon
`#CF6679`, because the Eggplant Wash identity is not implemented yet — it arrives
with Epic 3. Reconcile then.


## Entry 15 — Release control dropped; list title enlarged (2026-09-03)

At the user's direction:

- **The calendar list's title is three larger** than the row's own size
  (`calc(1em + 3px)`, so it tracks whatever the list uses rather than pinning a
  literal that would drift if the base changed).
- **The "I'm someone else" release control is removed** from the name dropdown.
  Switching is still possible — and is the real case — by picking a different name;
  what is gone is returning to *nobody*, which only made the app read-only for the
  person who chose it. `release()` stays in the composable: it is still how a claim
  naming a since-removed invitee gets cleared from storage. FR-10 updated in the
  spec and epics, and the EXPERIENCE.md open question about releasing is resolved
  rather than left hanging.


## Entry 16 — A past day opens; the claim prompt moves inside the sheet (2026-09-03)

Two corrections forced by building Epic 4 on top of Epic 3.

**Past days must be openable.** Story 3.5 made a past cell `disabled`, which read
as the right kind of "frozen" until Story 4.1 required an admin to mark a *past*
day chosen — unreachable through a disabled cell. The rule is now: a past day
cannot be **answered**, but can be **opened**. It is dimmed, its sheet says the day
has been and gone, and it still shows who was there — which turns out to be the
point of keeping past votes at all.

**The claim prompt moved into the sheet.** Story 3.2 had an unclaimed tap open the
name dropdown instead of the sheet. That made the sheet unreachable for an admin
who has not claimed a name — likely, since an admin need not be an invitee, so they
could never mark a day chosen. The sheet now always opens, and the "say who you
are" prompt sits inside it in place of the answer controls. The teaching moment
survives, one step later and in the context of the day just tapped.

FR-16 reworded in the spec and epics from "cannot be toggled" to "cannot be voted
on", and the EXPERIENCE.md component and interaction notes updated to match.


## Entry 17 — The wash reaches the list and the dialogs (2026-09-03)

Epic 3 themed the month and left everything around it on the dock's Carbon theme,
which the user asked to finish.

**Dialogs needed a second token root.** Quasar teleports `q-dialog` content to the
body, so a dialog card sits *outside* `.kdh-app` and inherited nothing — every
dialog dropped back to Carbon mid-flow. The tokens are now declared on
`.kdh-app, .kdh-panel`, and every KDH dialog card carries `kdh-panel`.

**Buttons revealed a real gap in DESIGN.md: KDH has no accent hue.** The spine
defines the wash and the invitee palette and stops, so the primary buttons were
still the dock's gold. Rather than invent an accent, a primary action is now a
raised surface (`wash-3` with `ink-hi`) and a destructive one is `danger` text on
transparent. That is the most faithful reading of "one idea carries colour" — a
coloured button would be a second thing competing with the only thing that
matters. Written into DESIGN.md under Components.

**`danger` is now genuinely a KDH token** rather than Carbon's negative borrowed by
accident, which is how entry 14 left it. The value is unchanged; the meaning is not.


## Entry 18 — The decided day goes gold; the count grows (2026-09-03)

At the user's direction:

- **The coverage count is one size larger** (8.5px → 10px). It is the backstop for
  two wash steps that look alike, so it was doing important work at a size chosen
  for decoration.
- **The chosen day's date is bold and gold**, with the diamond kept alongside.

Gold is a **third colour dimension** and was, until now, explicitly forbidden by
this spine ("never the dock's gold"). That rule was protecting against a second
thing competing for attention as an *interactive* accent — which gold still is
everywhere else on the dock. Used for "this is the decided day" it says something
the dock's gold never says, and it does not compete with the wash because it marks
one cell rather than ranking them. The Do/Don't is narrowed accordingly rather than
deleted.

Two golds, because one is not legible everywhere: `gold` on the dark end of the
ramp, `gold-deep` on `wash-5`/`wash-6` where the bright one washes out against pale
lilac — the same reason `ink-on-light` exists.

The diamond stays. The gold now carries the message, but NFR-6 says colour is never
the only signal, and someone who cannot separate gold from lilac still needs to see
which day was picked.


## Entry 19 — The decided day gets a ring and a glow (2026-09-03)

The first pass at gold was not loud enough. Brightened to `#FFD54A` and given a
glow, at the user's direction.

**The glow had to go on the cell, not only the date.** The chosen day is usually
the *full-coverage* day, which sits at `wash-6` — pale lilac — where bright gold
text has almost no contrast and a gold halo is mud. Text glow alone would have
failed on precisely the day it exists for. So the cell carries a gold ring and
outer glow, which reads at every step, and the text glow is an extra that only the
dark steps get.

Two smaller consequences, both invisible until they are wrong:

- **The chosen cell is lifted a layer.** Grid siblings paint in document order, so
  without a `z-index` the glow is covered by the background of every later cell in
  the month.
- **Chosen and provisional compose.** Both were single `box-shadow` rules, so one
  would have silently replaced the other on a day that is both. They are now one
  declaration — hairline inset, ring outside — with a test pinning that a day can
  wear both marks.


## Entry 20 — One gold everywhere, and bigger numerals (2026-09-03)

At the user's direction:

- **The date and the count each go up a size again** — 12.5px → 14px and
  10px → 11.5px. Both were originally sized as ornament on a cell whose whole job
  is to be read at arm's length.
- **The chosen day's date is the bright gold at every step of the ramp.** Entry 19
  had dropped it to `gold-deep` on `wash-5`/`wash-6`; the mark then looked like two
  different marks depending on how many people were free.

**This is a knowing accessibility trade.** Bright gold on pale lilac does not reach
4.5:1, which NFR-6 otherwise requires. Legibility comes from a dark halo behind the
glyph rather than from dulling the colour, and — the part that makes it acceptable
— the marking never rests on that text: the cell's gold ring, the diamond, and the
cell's accessible name each say the day is chosen independently. Recorded in
DESIGN.md's Do's and Don'ts as a named exception rather than left as a quiet
violation.

`gold-deep` survives for the diamond on the pale steps, where a bright gold shape
would disappear.


## Entry 21 — A crown replaces the diamond (2026-09-03)

At the user's direction, with the explicit requirement that the date stay aligned
with the rest of the month.

**The crown's slot is reserved in every cell, crowned or not.** Placing it in flow
only on chosen days would have pushed that one date down out of line with its
neighbours; positioning it absolutely would have overlapped the date in a 44px
cell. A fixed-height slot in all cells costs a few pixels of headroom everywhere
and guarantees the alignment. Pinned by a test.

**One crown, not two.** It is filled gold with a dark stroke, so it reads on the
pale top of the ramp as well as the dark bottom — the same approach the gold date
takes, rather than the darker variant the diamond used.

The crown remains the signal that does not depend on colour (NFR-6).


## Entry 22 — Hovering the count shows who voted (2026-09-03)

At the user's direction, with the mobile equivalent explicitly deferred by them.

The count is the one number in the cell that raises a question — *which* four? —
so it is the right thing to hang the answer on. The list is the day sheet's roster
content, in roster order, with if-needed people italicised rather than recoloured,
because their colour has to keep meaning *them*.

**Hover-only is a real limitation and is fine here.** KDH is phone-first, and a
phone has no hover, so on the target device this does nothing. It is still worth
having: the calendar is perfectly usable on a laptop, this is where a glance is
cheapest, and the same list is one tap away in the sheet regardless. A long-press
equivalent was not added — it would compete with the tap that opens the sheet,
which is the more important gesture. Logged as an open question rather than a
missing feature.

The cell's coverage number now has its own hook so tests can assert it without
picking up the floating list's text.
